Flotation Machine

Product OverviewProduct Overview

The Flotation Machine is a mechanical device that accomplishes the flotation process. In the flotation machine, chemically treated pulp is agitated and aerated, causing certain mineral particles to selectively attach to air bubbles, float to the pulp surface, and be scraped off as froth product (concentrate), while the remainder stays in the pulp (tailings), achieving mineral separation. This series covers four types of mechanical agitation flotation machines: XJ (A-type), XJZ (improved type), SF, and JJF, with effective volumes from 0.13-20 m³, suitable for large, medium, and small beneficiation plants.

Working PrincipleWorking Principle

1

Chemical Conditioning

Flotation reagents such as collectors, frothers, and modifiers are added to the pulp, making the target mineral surface hydrophobic and the gangue mineral surface hydrophilic, creating conditions for subsequent bubble attachment.

2

Aeration & Agitation

The impeller rotates at high speed, creating negative pressure that draws in air. Air is sheared into fine bubbles (diameter 0.5-2mm) dispersed in the pulp. Simultaneously, agitation ensures thorough contact between reagents and minerals.

3

Bubble Mineralization

Hydrophobic mineral particles collide with and attach to bubble surfaces, forming mineralized bubbles. The bubbles carry mineral particles upward to the pulp surface under buoyancy.

4

Froth Scraping & Collection

Mineralized bubbles rise to the surface forming a froth layer. The scraper rotates to scrape off the froth as concentrate. Hydrophilic gangue minerals remain in the pulp and are discharged through the tailings outlet.

Selection AdviceSelection Advice

  1. Select Series by Mineral Type & Scale: Small to medium plants (<1000 t/d), recommend XJ/XJZ type for cost-effectiveness. Large to medium plants (1000-10000 t/d), recommend SF/JJF type for high capacity and fast flotation speed.
  2. XJ vs XJZ: For new projects,Priority choose XJZ improved type. The dual-cone impeller provides better pulp circulation with about 15% energy reduction, and optimized cell design reduces dead zones.
  3. SF vs JJF Combination: Use SF type for suction cells (strong pulp self-aspiration), JJF type for direct-flow cells (stable level, thick froth). Combined unit flotation efficiency is 5-10% higher than single model alone.
  4. Cell Count Determination: Based on flotation time determined by laboratory tests (roughing typically 3-5 cells, cleaning 2-3 cells, scavenging 4-6 cells) and effective volume per cell, calculate total cells. Reserve 1-2 cells as margin in actual selection.
